14-Day Market Validation Playbook

Do Bangladesh-based small and medium online sellers have a recurring, meaningful cash-on-delivery (COD) courier reconciliation problem worth solving?

Status at issue: No interviews, surveys or demos have been conducted for this plan. No results, market sizes or customer feedback are claimed in this document. The P0 disposable prototype is complete (47 tests passed, 0 failed, as last reported) and is used here only with synthetic data. No production (M1) work is authorised by this playbook.

Who this is for

Bangladesh-based online sellers who sell on Facebook, their own website, WooCommerce, Shopify or a mix, and who receive COD settlements from one or more of Steadfast, Pathao, Paperfly, RedX and eCourier. Do not assume those couriers settle, charge or report in the same way.

What you will have after 14 days

  • A completed evidence tracker: one row per seller, coded the same way every time.
  • A competitor log based on hands-on checks, not marketing pages alone.
  • A Day 7 and a Day 14 decision - Continue, Revise the hypothesis, Narrow the market, or Stop - taken against thresholds set in this document before fieldwork starts.
How to use this playbook: Work through Sections 1-4 before contacting anyone. Run the days in Section 5 in order. Score the gates in Section 6 exactly as written, even if the answer is Stop. Keep every note in the templates in Section 10.

Money language used throughout: expected (what the seller expected to collect and be paid), courier-reported (what the courier statement says), and bank-confirmed (what actually arrived) are three different things and are never merged. An unexplained difference is recorded as unexplained - never as a confirmed loss.

1. The problem hypothesis - stated so it can fail

Hypothesis: A meaningful share of Bangladesh-based small and medium online sellers who use COD couriers regularly spend significant time checking courier settlements by hand, regularly find differences they cannot explain from the statement alone (under-collection, unexpected delivery or return fees, or a payout that does not match the statement), and have no current workflow that reliably separates expected, courier-reported and bank-confirmed amounts at shipment level.

If sellers instead check settlements quickly, rarely find unexplained differences, or already have a tool or routine that catches them, the hypothesis fails - and that is a valid, useful result of these 14 days.

What counts as the problem, in the seller's own terms

  • Time: minutes or hours spent per settlement cycle checking statements against orders and bank or mobile-money receipts.
  • Frequency: how often a difference appears that the seller cannot explain from the documents they have.
  • Consequence: money queried with a courier, money written off, delayed decisions, or distrust of their own profit figures.
  • Workaround cost: a staff member, an accountant, a spreadsheet routine, or a paid tool already doing this job.
Do not lead. Never describe CourierLedger, its findings (collection variance, fee variance, statement inconsistency), or the words "underpaid" or "loss" before the seller has described their last settlement in their own words. The interview guide in Section 4 is written to prevent this.

Scope boundaries for validation

  • In scope: sellers with COD volume in the last 30 days who personally check, or supervise checking of, courier settlements.
  • Out of scope: prepaid-only sellers, courier staff, and anyone asked to share real financial records. No real merchant data goes into the P0 prototype.
  • Public courier pricing pages are not evidence of any individual seller's contract. Rates are only recorded as the seller describes their own merchant-confirmed terms.

4. Interview guide - the last settlement, in detail (35-40 min)

Open with: "I am researching how online sellers check courier payments. I am not selling anything in this call. I will ask about the last time you did it, step by step. Please do not share account numbers, customer details or full statements." Record only with permission; otherwise take notes in the template in Section 10.

A. Context (5 min)

Q1. What do you sell, and through which channels?
Q2. Which couriers do you use now, and why those?
Q3. Roughly how many COD shipments in a normal month?

B. Walk through the last settlement (15 min) - the core of the interview

Q4. Think of the most recent courier payment you checked. Which courier, and what period did it cover?
Q5. What documents or screens did you have open? (courier dashboard, statement file, order list, bank or bKash record, notebook, spreadsheet)
Q6. Walk me through it, step by step, from receiving the payment to deciding it was right - or not right.
Q7. How often does this checking happen?
Q8. Who else is involved, if anyone?
Q9. How long did that last check take, start to finish? What took the longest?
Q10. How do you keep track of orders that were delivered, partially delivered, returned or cancelled?

C. Fees and differences (10 min) - ask neutrally

Q11. On that statement, which charges appeared? How do you know each one is correct?
Q12. In the last three settlement cycles, was there any amount that did not match what you expected? Please describe the most recent one.
Q13. What did you do about it? What did the courier say or do?
Q14. Have you ever left a difference unresolved? What happened to that amount in your books?
Q15. How were your delivery, COD and return charges agreed with the courier? Where is that written down?
Q16. For a returned parcel, what are you charged, and how do you check it?
Q17. For a partial delivery, how is the amount to collect decided, and how do you check it?
Q18. How do return fees owed to a courier get settled, if not from a COD payout?

4. Interview guide (continued)

D. Bank verification and records (5 min)

Q19. How do you confirm the money actually reached your bank or mobile-money account?
Q20. Can you connect a bank credit to the specific shipments in a statement? How?
Q21. Where do you keep the record afterwards, and for how long?

E. Current tools and costs (5 min) - no leading

Q22. What tools do you use for this today - spreadsheet, accounting software, courier dashboard, notebook, something else? What does each one do well or badly?
Q23. Have you ever paid for help with this - staff time, an accountant, software? What did it cost, roughly?
Q24. If this checking took no time at all, what would you do with that time? (Listen; do not price-anchor.)
Q25. Have you tried anything to make this easier in the last six months? What happened?
Q26. What, if anything, have you spent money on in the last six months to reduce errors or time in your delivery and payments process?
Never ask: "Would you use a tool that...?", "Would you pay X taka for...?", or "Do you like this idea?" Hypothetical enthusiasm is not evidence. Willingness to pay is inferred only from past spending (Q23, Q26), time already spent, and a concrete commitment at the end: agreeing to a prototype test session with a date, or introducing another seller.

F. Close (3 min)

Q27. Is there a part of checking courier payments I should have asked about and did not?
Q28. May I invite you to a short test session using example data only - no real statements needed? (If yes, book the date now.)
Q29. Who else checks courier payments carefully and might talk to me?

Immediately after each interview (10 min, non-negotiable): complete one tracker row, code the metrics in Section 7, save two verbatim quotes (with permission), and write one sentence: "The strongest evidence for / against the hypothesis in this interview was..." Separate what the seller said from what you think it means.

Bangla prompts for key money terms (use if the seller prefers Bangla)

  • Expected amount - expected taka (apni ja asha korechilen); courier-reported - courier statement-e ja lekha ache; bank-confirmed - bank-e ja joma hoyeche.
  • Unexplained difference - hishebe meleni emon parthokko. Do not translate this as "loss" (khoti) unless the seller uses that word first.

9. Prototype demonstration - synthetic data only (15 min)

Hard rule: Run the P0 prototype only with its synthetic demo and edge-case datasets. Never upload a seller's real statement, even if they offer it. If a seller wants to share an example, accept only: column headers with values removed, courier rule documentation, or a synthetic reproduction they approve - each logged with consent in the tracker.

Script - narrate the question, not the verdict

  1. CSV upload (2 min). Load the synthetic demo dataset. Say: "This is invented example data." Ask: "Does this look like the file or screen you start from?"
  2. Column mapping (2 min). Show how columns are matched to fields. Ask what their statement calls the same things.
  3. Results table (3 min). Show matched, open, mismatch, needs-review and matched-with-tolerance outcomes. Ask the seller to explain in their words what each row is telling them - their interpretation is the data.
  4. Under-collection example (2 min). Open a shipment where courier-reported collected COD is below expected collectible COD. Note: the contractual payout can still be matched - watch whether the seller finds that distinction clear or confusing.
  5. Fee discrepancy example (2 min). Open a fee/tax variance case. Ask: "What would you do next with this?"
  6. Statement inconsistency example (2 min). Open a case where the statement's own arithmetic does not hold. Record whether the seller has seen this pattern before (yes/no, no prompting).
  7. Data-quality warnings (2 min). Show a missing-contract case and a blank-versus-zero fee case. Ask whether the warning matches how they would want unknown values treated.

Observe, do not score enthusiasm

  • Record: could the seller explain each finding back correctly? Where did they hesitate or misread?
  • Record any finding they said they would act on, and the action they named.
  • Close with the only commitment question that counts: "May I follow up with a test using example data shaped like your statement headers?" Book a date or record a no.

Appendix - Outreach message and note-taking discipline

Outreach message (adapt the channel line; send no product description)

"Hello, I am researching how online sellers in Bangladesh check the payments they receive from courier companies for cash-on-delivery orders. I am speaking with sellers for 30-40 minutes about how they did this most recently. I am not selling anything, and you would not need to share any account details or statements. Would you be open to a short conversation this week? I can work around your schedule."

If asked what the research is for, answer honestly and briefly: "To understand whether the checking process is a real problem for sellers, before deciding whether anything should be built for it." Do not name features, prices or timelines.

Note-taking discipline

  • Write what the seller did and said, in order. Interpretation goes in the separate "strongest evidence" sentence, never mixed into the account.
  • Numbers are recorded exactly as stated, with the question number beside them, so the tracker coding can be audited.
  • Blank fields stay blank. An unanswered question is data; never fill a gap from a later guess.
  • At each gate, re-read two disconfirming interviews in full before scoring. The gates exist to stop a liked idea, not to ratify one.

What happens after Day 14

The final memo states the decision, the tracker numbers behind it, the competitor gaps actually verified, and the strongest disconfirming evidence. If the decision is Continue, the memo proposes the next validation step only. Production development, paid hosting, live courier integration and real merchant data all remain out of scope until separately and explicitly approved.

End of playbook. Start at Day 1. Do not skip the gates.
