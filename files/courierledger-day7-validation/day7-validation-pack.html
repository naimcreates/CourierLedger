Money language (playbook §1, kept throughout): expected (what the seller expected to collect and be paid), courier-reported (what the courier statement says), and bank-confirmed (what actually arrived) are three different things and are never merged. An unexplained difference is recorded as unexplained — never as a confirmed loss. Bangla prompts from the playbook: expected amount — apni ja asha korechilen; courier-reported — courier statement-e ja lekha ache; bank-confirmed — bank-e ja joma hoyeche; unexplained difference — hishebe meleni emon parthokko. Do not translate it as “loss” (khoti) unless the seller uses that word first.

Hard rules for the whole week. (1) No real merchant financial data anywhere — not in the prototype, not in notes, not in the tracker. (2) Never ask for a seller's courier login, password, or account access. (3) Never describe CourierLedger's findings, features, prices or timelines before the seller has described their last settlement in their own words. (4) Do not contact sellers automatically — every message is sent by Naim, by hand, at most twice per seller. (5) Do not mark any gate as passed without tracker evidence. (6) A positive comment (“great idea”) counts for nothing in either gate.

1. Seller recruitment strategy

1.1 Who we are looking for (and who we are not)

Priority, in order: (1) Facebook-first online sellers who take COD orders and check courier payments themselves; (2) sellers using two or more couriers (Steadfast, Pathao, Paperfly, RedX, eCourier) — multi-courier sellers carry the format-and-rules multiplication the hypothesis depends on (assumption A6); (3) sellers doing regular COD volume — screening keeps sellers with 20+ COD shipments in the last 30 days (playbook S3); (4) sellers who reconcile manually in a spreadsheet, notebook, or by hand — the incumbent workflow CourierLedger must beat; (5) small Shopify / WooCommerce merchants, because several existing tools are Shopify-only and the open question (A7) is whether non-Shopify / Facebook-first sellers are served at all.

Not the target this week: prepaid-only sellers; sellers whose settlement checking is fully outsourced with no seller involvement; courier or software sellers; sellers with no COD in the last 30 days. Disqualifying them is not failure — a high disqualify rate is itself evidence about the market, and every disqualification reason is logged in Screening_Log.

Day 7 target mix (from the playbook's 14-day mix, scaled to 8): 8 completed interviews · at least 3 couriers represented · at least 4 Facebook-first sellers · a spread of volumes (under 100, 100–500, over 500 shipments/month). The mix is a recruitment target, not a result.

1.5 Incentives — a decision for Naim, not assumed

No incentive is promised in any message in this pack. Whether to offer one (for example mobile recharge or a small thank-you after the interview) is Naim's decision, and it must be decided before the first message goes out — changing it mid-week would bias who agrees. Considerations: an incentive may raise response rates but can also attract sellers who do not reconcile; if one is offered, record it in the Day 7 report and keep the amount the same for every seller. Never offer a discount on, or free use of, CourierLedger — the product is not for sale and not production-ready.

2. Interview guide — 15–20 minutes, behaviour not validation

What this guide is: a shortened version of the playbook's 35–40 minute guide (Q1–Q29) for the Day 7 pace — two interviews a day plus same-day coding. It keeps the core of the method (walk through the last settlement, in detail) and defers the rest to Days 8–14, when the full guide is used. Reuse map: every question below names the playbook question it comes from. Nothing here contradicts the playbook; where a playbook question is shortened, the note says so.

2.1 Before the call (2 minutes, not counted in the 15–20)

  • Confirm the seller passed screening (S1–S8) and has a Screening_Log row with an Interview ID (V-01, V-02, …) already assigned.
  • Have the interview-notes template open (day7-interview-notes-template.md) and the tracker ready. Do not take notes in a separate app and copy later — same-day coding is non-negotiable (playbook §4).
  • Check the consent wording (§2.2) and the Bangla money-term prompts. If recording, ask permission first; if they say no, take written notes only.

2.2 Opening + consent (1 minute)

“I am researching how online sellers check courier payments. I am not selling anything in this call. I will ask about the last time you did it, step by step — about 15 to 20 minutes. Please do not share account numbers, customer details or full statements. Is that okay, and may I take notes?”

If they ask what the research is for: “To understand whether checking courier payments is a real problem for sellers, before deciding whether anything should be built for it.” Then start. Do not name CourierLedger, its findings, or any feature before the seller has described their last settlement.

2.3 The questions (15–20 minutes)

A. Business context — 2 minutes

D1. What do you sell, and through which channels? (Playbook Q1.)

D2. Which couriers do you use now, and why those? (Playbook Q2.)

D3. Roughly how many COD shipments in a normal month — under 100, 100–500, or over 500? (Playbook Q3; band only, never exact revenue.)

B. Walk through the last settlement — 7 minutes (the core; do not rush this)

D4. Think of the most recent courier payment you checked. Which courier, and what period did it cover? (Playbook Q4.)

D5. Walk me through it, step by step, from receiving the payment to deciding it was right — or not right. What did you have open — courier dashboard, statement file, order list, bank or bKash record, notebook, spreadsheet? (Playbook Q5 + Q6 combined.)

D6. How long did that last check take, start to finish? What took the longest? And how often does this checking happen — every settlement, weekly, monthly? (Playbook Q9 + Q7 combined. Record the number they give; do not round up.)

D7. Who does this checking — you, a team member, an accountant? How do you keep track of orders that were delivered, partially delivered, returned or cancelled? (Playbook Q8 + Q10 combined.)

C. Differences and pain — 4 minutes (ask neutrally; never use the word “loss”)

D8. In the last three settlement cycles, was there any amount that did not match what you expected? Please describe the most recent one. (Playbook Q12. This is the question the ≥50% target is measured from. If they cannot recall, record “unknown” — never 0.)

D9. What did you do about it? What did the courier say or do? Have you ever left a difference unresolved — and what happened to that amount in your books? (Playbook Q13 + Q14 combined.)

D. Bank check and current tools — 3 minutes

D10. How do you confirm the money actually reached your bank or mobile-money account? Can you connect a bank credit to the specific shipments in a statement? (Playbook Q19 + Q20 combined; Q21 deferred to Days 8–14.)

D11. What tools do you use for this today — spreadsheet, accounting software, courier dashboard, notebook, something else? What does each one do well or badly? Have you ever paid for help with this — staff time, an accountant, software — and what did it cost, roughly? (Playbook Q22 + Q23 combined. Past spend only; never ask what they would pay.)

E. Close — 2 minutes

D12. Two things before we finish. First — is there a part of checking courier payments I should have asked about and did not? (Playbook Q27.) Second — may I invite you to a short test session using example data only, no real statements needed? If yes, let us book the date now. And who else checks courier payments carefully and might talk to me? (Playbook Q28 + Q29 combined. A booked date is a commitment; verbal interest is not.)

2.4 What was deferred to Days 8–14 (full 35–40 minute guide)

Playbook Q11 (which charges appeared, how each is verified), Q15 (how charges were agreed / where written down), Q16–Q18 (returned-parcel charges, partial-delivery amounts, return fees owed outside a payout), Q21 (record-keeping afterwards), Q24–Q26 (what freed-up time would be used for, what they tried in the last six months, what they spent to reduce errors). These matter — especially Q15–Q18, which are the only route to merchant-confirmed courier evidence (OD-12, B-1 in the original gating sense) — but they do not fit a 15–20 minute first conversation, and the Day 7 gate does not need them. Do not improvise them into a Day 7 interview; use the full guide from Day 8.

2.5 Interviewer notes — how to run it well

  • Probe with the last example, not with opinions. “Can you give me the most recent time that happened?” beats “Does that happen often?” If they generalise (“couriers always cheat”), ask for the last specific case, with the courier name and the settlement period.
  • Write what they did and said, in order. Interpretation goes in the separate “strongest evidence” sentence after the interview, never mixed into the account. Numbers are recorded exactly as stated, with the question number beside them, so tracker coding can be audited.
  • Blank fields stay blank. An unanswered question is data; never fill a gap from a later guess, and never convert “don't know” into 0 — in the tracker, a blank amount is unknown.
  • When to stop. If the seller gets uncomfortable, offers real statements, or asks for help with a live dispute: stop that line, thank them, and note it. Do not accept files, do not look at their courier account, do not advise on a live dispute.
  • Never ask: “Would you use a tool that…?”, “Would you pay X taka for…?”, “Do you like this idea?” Hypothetical enthusiasm is not evidence. Willingness to test is measured only by a booked demo/test date (D12), and willingness to pay only by past spend (D11).
  • After every interview (10 minutes, non-negotiable): complete one Interview_Log row, log at least two Evidence_Log items (one for and one against the hypothesis where they exist), save two verbatim quotes (with permission), and write one sentence: “The strongest evidence for / against the hypothesis in this interview was …”. Record disconfirming interviews with the same care as confirming ones — they decide the gates.

4. Synthetic demo script — what the M1 build can actually show

Hard rules. Synthetic data only — the P0 demo dataset and the M1 generic statement fixture. Never upload a seller's real statement, even if they offer it; if they offer, stop, thank them, switch back to the synthetic dataset, and note the offer in Demo_Log (do not accept the file). Never ask for courier logins or account access. Never present synthetic results as customer findings. Never claim the demo shows a feature that is still pending. Say at the start, out loud: “This is invented example data. Nothing here is a real seller's money.”

Which product to demo: the M1 local build carries the end-to-end flow (import → run → settlement → bank receipt → variance). The P0 prototype carries the named synthetic examples for steps 4–7 (its 14-shipment demo dataset). Use M1 for steps 1–3 and 8, P0 for steps 4–7 — and say which one is on screen at each step. Both run locally only; there is no public link. A demo therefore happens on Naim's screen (in person or screen-share), not by sending the seller a link.

In practice: 8 interviews done, ≥50% with an unexplained difference, and either the time or the willingness-to-test criterion also met.
B. Adjust positioningThe problem exists, but the target segment, workflow or proposition needs refinement. (Includes the playbook's “Narrow the target market”: if the signal concentrates in one segment — e.g. only sellers over 500 shipments/month, or only multi-courier sellers — Days 8–14 are rewritten for that segment.)A real signal exists but is concentrated or partial: e.g. differences exist but sellers resolve them inside the courier dashboard (then the hypothesis, not the effort, is wrong — revise the proposition); or the time cost is real only above a volume band; or willingness to test is there but only for a different workflow (e.g. batch totals, not shipment lines — assumption A11). Name the segment and the change.
C. Reconsider the hypothesisInterview evidence does not support the assumed problem strongly enough. (Includes the playbook's “Stop”: if a Stop signal fired, the honest decision is to stop this line and reconsider, not to push on.)Weak or absent signal: few or no unexplained differences, median time under 15 minutes, no test agreements, or an existing tool demonstrably covering the segment (competitor gap refuted hands-on). The report must name the disconfirming evidence, not just the absence of confirming evidence.

What “Continue” does and does not mean: it justifies a next validation step (for example a concierge test with consented, redacted data). It does not authorise production development (M1), paid hosting, or collecting real merchant financial data. It does not claim product-market fit — eight interviews cannot do that, however positive they are.

6.3 Incomplete sample — the rule

If fewer than 8 interviews are completed by Day 7, the scorecard is scored anyway and the decision is recorded as incomplete: report the actual count beside every average and share (the tracker already does this — averages over 2–3 interviews are anecdotes, not market evidence), state which criteria could not be judged, and choose between B (extend recruitment into Days 8–14 and re-gate) and C (the outreach itself showed the segment is unreachable or uninterested, which is evidence). Never present a partial sample as if it were the full gate.

Change log rule (tracker): if any threshold was changed after interviews began, record the date, old value, new value and reason in the scorecard's Decision note. An unlogged change invalidates the gate.

7. Market-validation report template

The fillable template is the companion file day7-report-template.md (use it at Day 7, and again at Day 14 with the Day 14 scorecard). Its sections, in order: (1) what was done — days, contacts made, screened, eligible, booked, completed, with the funnel's real rates beside the assumed rates from §1.3; (2) interviews table — Interview IDs only (V-01…), date, channel, segment, volume band, couriers, minutes/cycle, unexplained differences in last 3 cycles, current method, willing to test (date or no); (3) metrics vs targets — the seven scorecard criteria with Actual, Met?, and the evidence note naming the interview/evidence IDs behind each; (4) evidence — anonymised quotes (seller codes only) and the strongest evidence for and against, separately; (5) what the evidence does and does not prove — including the sample-size limit and which segments were not reached; (6) decision (A / B / C) with reasoning, including the contradicting evidence weighed; (7) next steps for Days 8–14 (or the stop/reconsider plan). All fields are empty in the template. The one labelled example row in the playbook's tracker README is the only example anywhere; nothing else in the template is pre-filled.
